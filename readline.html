KLM to look for a replacement for its Boeing 777-300ERs sooner rather than later.



Air France-KLM is looking to place another widebody aircraft order to replace its Boeing 777 300ERs with a decision likely to come in the near term.

CEO Ben Smith flagged out that a decision must come sooner rather than later due to the production backlog from both Boeing and Airbus with a tight orderbook from both the Airbus A350 1000 and the Boeing 777-9 through the 2030s. The need for new jets has been highly influenced by the airline's fleet strategy, which aims to modernize its long-haul fleet and improve fuel efficiency.

KLM evaluates the Airbus A350 1000 and Boeing 777-9


Smith evaluated both options as future replacements for the Boeing 777-300ER, which is currently the backbone of KLM's long-haul fleet. The Airbus A350 1000 is a newer aircraft that offers improved fuel efficiency and a longer range than the 777-300ER, while the Boeing 777-9 is a larger aircraft that can carry more passengers and cargo.
On time delivery is also a key factor in the decision-making process, as both aircraft have long lead times for production and delivery. The A350 1000 is already in service with several airlines, while the 777-9 is still in development and has not yet entered service. This could impact KLM's ability to receive the aircraft in a timely manner and meet its operational needs. Smith pointed out two key factors that will influence the decision: the need for a larger aircraft to replace the 777-300ER and the need for a more fuel-efficient aircraft to reduce operating costs. While the A350 1000 is larger than the 777-300ER, the 777-9 is more fuel-efficient in terms of fuel burn per seat. On the other hand, the A350 1000 has a longer range than the 777-9, which could be an important factor for KLM's long-haul operations.
While both aircraft have their advantages and disadvantages, KLM will need to carefully evaluate its options and make a decision that aligns with its long-term strategy and goals.
